Picture puzzles

Pick a library picture, upload an image you own, or draw one square by square. Gridbook turns it into a grid and proves it can be solved one line at a time.

How it works

From idea to KDP upload in three steps

  1. Pick a template

    Large print word search, sudoku from easy to expert, picture logic, a brain-games mix, a kids’ activity book, or a blank book. Choose the trim size and paper.

  2. Generate and tweak

    Puzzles appear as you go. Change grid sizes, themes and difficulty, regenerate a single puzzle or lock the ones you like. The preview shows every page as it will print.

  3. Export the interior and cover template

    Preflight checks the book against KDP’s specs, then you download the interior PDF with its answer key and a cover template with bleed, spine and barcode area.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

Will KDP accept the files?

Gridbook builds to KDP’s published paperback specs: trim sizes, the gutter for your page count, minimum margins, bleed, embedded fonts and grayscale ink. A preflight check runs before every export. Nobody but KDP can promise a review outcome, because KDP also reviews content and listing details. If KDP rejects a Pro interior for a technical-spec reason, email us within 30 days for a full refund. Free proof PDFs carry a proof header and footer and are not meant for upload.

Are the puzzles AI-generated?

No. Every puzzle is made by an algorithm running on your device: words are placed and checked by code, sudoku are built and solved by a solver that proves there is one answer, mazes are carved so there is one route, and nonograms are checked by a line solver. The same settings and seed always give the same puzzle. If you write your own word lists with an AI chatbot, check KDP’s content guidelines on AI disclosure.

Can I sell what I make?

Yes, with Pro (Lifetime, or a 7-Day Pass while it runs). The commercial-use license covers KDP paperbacks, printables sold on Etsy or Gumroad, and paid newsletters. Books you export with Pro stay yours to sell after a pass ends. You may not resell the built-in word lists as standalone lists. Free exports are for proofing and for personal or classroom use. The License page has the full terms.

Can I sell printables on Etsy?

Yes, with Pro. Export clean printable PDFs in US Letter or A4, with as many puzzles as you like, and sell them as your own product. Free printables carry a “Free printable from gridbookstudio.com” footer, hold up to 10 puzzles per file and are for personal and classroom use.

Does it work offline?

Partly. Once the Studio has loaded, you can keep editing if your connection drops, because puzzles and previews are made in your browser. Exporting may need the connection if it drops in the first few seconds, because the export tools load in the background just after the Studio opens. Keep the tab open: reloading the page needs the network too. Nothing you make is ever uploaded. What does go online is the site’s own files, the license check and checkout with Gumroad, and cookieless page-view counts; the Privacy page has the details.

What does the license check send?

Only your license key, with the ID of the product it belongs to, sent to Gumroad’s license API. No puzzles, book content or files are sent anywhere. The check runs when you activate and then about once a week. If Gumroad can’t be reached, Pro keeps working for up to 30 days.

What about refunds?

Ask within 30 days of your purchase and we refund it in full, for any reason. That includes the KDP promise: if KDP rejects your Pro interior for a technical-spec reason, you get your money back and we fix the cause. Refunds go back to your original payment method through Gumroad. You can also try everything in the free edition first: it builds and previews the same books.

How large is large print?

On puzzle pages, the Large Print theme keeps text at 16 pt or larger, word-search letters at 18 pt or larger and sudoku digits at 22 pt or larger, with heavier grid lines. Nonogram clue numbers inside a puzzle may go down to 14 pt. The Studio warns you if a layout would push a puzzle below these sizes. Answer keys are the exception: they fit several solved grids on a page, so their letters are smaller. KDP doesn’t define large print; these sizes follow common practice for large-print puzzle books.
