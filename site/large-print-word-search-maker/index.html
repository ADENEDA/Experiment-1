Guidance for senior readers

Large print is about more than big letters. Readers with age-related vision loss also need strong contrast, letters that cannot be mistaken for each other, and a page that is not crowded. Here is what the Large Print preset does, and what you can do when you print.

Type size and font

The Large Print preset prints grid letters at 18 pt or larger and the word list at 16 pt or larger, and the status line under the preview shows the exact sizes for the puzzle on screen. There is no official definition of large print; 18 pt is a common choice for large-print puzzles, so if you know your readers, ask them.

Everything is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface the Braille Institute created for low-vision readers. Its letters are drawn to be told apart easily, which matters when someone scans a grid one letter at a time. Grid letters and the word list are capitals.

Contrast

  • Pure black ink on white paper. No gray letters, tinted boxes or pictures behind the grid; answers are circled only in the answer key.
  • Turn off draft or toner-saving modes when you print. They print black as gray.
  • Print at Actual size or 100%, not “Fit to page”, which shrinks everything a little. Choose the paper you will print on: US Letter or A4.
  • Matte paper cuts glare under a reading lamp, and heavier paper (24 lb or 90 gsm) stops pen ink showing through.

Grid size per page

Keep to one puzzle per page and a grid of 15×15 or smaller. Bigger grids push the letters below 18 pt on a Letter or A4 page, so the Large Print preset stops at 15×15. This table shows the smallest letter size the tool printed across all 68 built-in themes with Easy directions.

Medium and Hard directions fit more words into the same grid, so the list takes more room and the letters come out a point or two smaller, still 18 pt or more.

Directions and words

Easy puzzles run every word across or down, the way we read, and suit most new or returning solvers. Medium adds diagonals; Hard adds backwards words in all eight directions. The 68 themes use everyday, family-friendly words with no brand names or proper nouns. With My own words you can make a puzzle for a club, a church group or a family reunion: type one word per line, and the tool keeps letters A to Z only (ICE CREAM becomes ICECREAM) and lists any word too long for the grid.

For activity coordinators and caregivers

Each puzzle sits alone on its page, with the answers on the last page so you can keep them back. A puzzle slipped into a clear sheet protector can be done with a dry-erase marker and wiped clean for the next person.

Make a printable in four steps

  1. Leave Print size on Large Print (18 pt+), or choose Standard for regular-size puzzles.
  2. Pick a theme, or choose My own words and type or paste your list.
  3. Choose a grid size and directions. 13×13 with Easy directions is a comfortable start.
  4. Pick US Letter or A4 and how many puzzles you need (up to 10), then download the free printable. The answers are on the last page.
