Why the gutter grows with page count

The inside margin, or gutter, is the margin on the edge of each page that goes into the binding. KDP paperbacks are glued at the spine, so they never open flat, and the thicker the book, the more of each page curves away into the fold. Text or a puzzle grid that runs too close to the spine is hard to read and hard to write in. So KDP sets the minimum inside margin by page count, and checks it when you upload your interior.

The 151, 301, 501 and 701-page steps

The minimum doesn’t grow smoothly. It jumps by 0.125 in (3.2 mm) at four page counts:

The count that matters is the final page count of the PDF you upload: title page, blank pages, notes pages and the answer key all count. That is how books most often fail the margin check. A puzzle book planned at 148 pages is fine with a 0.375 in gutter, then a four-page answer key takes it to 152, and every page needs 0.5 in.

There are two ways to stay safe. Set the inside margin for the step you expect to finish in, or reset the margins once the page count is final. Gridbook Studio does the second for you: it lays out the book, checks which step the final page count falls in, and lays the book out again if the step changed.

Outside, top and bottom margins, and bleed

Without bleed, the top, bottom and outside margins must be at least 0.25 in (6.4 mm). With bleed, the PDF page is 0.125 in wider and 0.25 in taller than the trim: bleed goes on the outside, top and bottom edges, never on the inside. The minimum then becomes 0.375 in (9.6 mm), measured from the edge of that larger page, which is the same 0.25 in from the trim line. Choose bleed only if something should print right to the edge of the paper. Puzzle books rarely need it, and KDP’s help notes that if even one page bleeds, the whole file must be set up with bleed.

The margins Gridbook uses, and why

KDP’s numbers are a floor, not a target. A grid that runs to 0.25 in from the trim looks cramped, and trimming varies slightly from copy to copy. Gridbook uses 0.6 in at the top and bottom, 0.5 in on the outside, and KDP’s gutter plus 0.125 in on the inside, so grids stay clear of the fold. Page numbers and running headers sit in the space between those margins and KDP’s minimum.

Typing the numbers into Word, Affinity or InDesign

Word measures margins from the edge of the paper and has no bleed setting, so with bleed you make the paper bigger. Word also has a separate Gutter field that it adds on top of the inside margin. KDP’s help suggests entering the same value in both fields; in Word that doubles the inside margin, which is safe but wastes space, so the settings above put the whole inside margin in Inside and 0 in Gutter. Affinity Publisher and InDesign set the page at the trim size and add bleed outside it, so their margins are measured from the trim. The settings above already account for that, and the spine width calculator does the same job for your cover.

Check your margins in four steps

  1. Choose the trim size you will select in KDP.
  2. Enter the final page count, including blank pages, notes pages and the answer key.
  3. Choose Bleed only if something on your pages prints right to the edge.
  4. Type the settings for your layout app, and check again if the page count crosses 151, 301, 501 or 701.
