How KDP works out spine width

Spine width depends on two things: the number of pages and the paper. Trim size doesn’t change it. KDP publishes a thickness per page for each paper, and the spine is simply the page count times that figure:

  • White paper: 0.002252 in (0.0572 mm) per page.
  • Cream paper: 0.0025 in (0.0635 mm) per page. Cream is a thicker sheet, so the same book has a wider spine.

A 120-page book on white paper has a spine of 120 × 0.002252 = 0.27024 in, which rounds to 0.27 in (6.86 mm). On cream it is exactly 0.3 in. Count every page in the interior PDF you upload, including the title page, blank pages, notes pages and the answer key, because KDP measures the file, not the content.

Full cover size, with bleed

KDP wants one PDF for the whole cover: back, spine and front side by side, with 0.125 in (3.2 mm) of bleed on the top, bottom and outside edges. The bleed is trimmed off, so your background has to run into it, or you’ll get a thin white edge.

Width is bleed + back + spine + front + bleed, and height is bleed + trim height + bleed. For a 6 × 9 in book with 120 white pages, that’s 0.125 + 6 + 0.27024 + 6 + 0.125 = 12.52024 in wide by 9.25 in tall. At 300 DPI, KDP’s minimum resolution for cover images, the canvas is 3756 × 2775 pixels. Pixel sizes here are rounded to the nearest whole pixel.

When you can put text on the spine

KDP’s cover help says “We only print spine text on books with more than 79 pages”. The same page also says “at least 79 pages” in two places, and Cover Creator needs 80. This calculator uses the strict reading, 80 pages or more, which satisfies all three.

Spine text also needs at least 0.0625 in (1.6 mm) clear of each fold, because every book binds slightly differently. On a thin spine that leaves very little room. At 80 white pages the spine is 0.18 in, so the text area is under 0.06 in, about 4 pt. A line of 10 pt type needs roughly 0.27 in of spine: 118 white pages or 106 cream ones.

Using the cover guide

The cover guide is a PDF or a 300 DPI PNG at the exact size of your cover. The gray band is the bleed, the solid line is the trim, and the dashed black lines are the spine folds. The dashed gray boxes are safe zones 0.25 in inside the trim. KDP’s minimum for cover text is 0.125 in, and the guide adds room for the 0.125 in production variance that KDP allows for.

The barcode box on the back is 2 × 1.2 in, a size widely used for KDP covers. We didn’t find it on the KDP page we read, so check KDP’s barcode help before you put artwork near it. Place the guide on the bottom layer in Canva, Affinity or Photoshop, design on top, and hide it before you export your cover PDF.

If you build the interior in Gridbook Studio, the export dialog makes the same guide from your book’s final page count, so the spine always matches the interior you upload. Pages also have to meet KDP’s margin rules: the margin and gutter calculator works those out.

Get your cover size in four steps

  1. Choose your trim size: the finished size of the book, the same one you pick in KDP.

  3. Choose the paper you will select in KDP: white or cream.
