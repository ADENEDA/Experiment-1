Atkinson Hyperlegible

Atkinson Hyperlegible was created for the Braille Institute of America to make letters easy to tell apart, which is why Gridbook Studio uses it for large-print puzzles. The Regular and Bold files are in fonts/, and the Studio embeds them in every PDF it exports.

Copyright 2020 Braille Institute of America, Inc. Licensed under the SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1. Read the full license text (OFL.txt).

The license lets the font be bundled and embedded, and states that “the requirement for fonts to remain under this license does not apply to any document created using the fonts or their derivatives.” Your books are not affected by the font license.

jsPDF

jsPDF 4.2.1 turns the Studio’s pages into PDF files on your device. Copyright (c) 2010-2025 James Hall and (c) 2015-2025 yWorks GmbH, with many contributors listed in the file. MIT License. Read the license text (jspdf-LICENSE.txt).

The jsPDF file also bundles small components under their own permissive licenses, among them pako 2.1.0 (MIT and Zlib), omggif (MIT) and a JPEG encoder (Copyright 2008 Adobe Systems Incorporated, BSD license). Their notices are kept inside the shipped file, vendor/jspdf-4.2.1.umd.min.js.

fflate

fflate 0.8.3 packs the ZIP files that Pro exports on your device: the book bundle (interior, cover guide, answer key, project file and a README with the KDP upload settings) and the per-page PNG images.

Phosphor Icons

The pictures in the nonogram library (the cat, the dog, the heart and the rest) are silhouettes from the “fill” style of Phosphor Icons. Each one was converted into puzzle grids at several sizes and checked to be solvable without guessing. The same shapes appear as small previews in the Studio and on this site.

For book makers

A printed nonogram is a coarse grid of squares derived from one of these shapes. We include the notice above, and you don’t need to credit anyone in your book. If you would like to be extra careful, add a line such as “Some puzzle pictures are based on Phosphor Icons (MIT License)” to your copyright page.
